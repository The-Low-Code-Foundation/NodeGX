# Rocket School (SSA edition): subjects, stages and content sources

Research briefing, 2026-10-14. Evidence grades used throughout:

- **strong**: an official curriculum, exam or assessment document that I opened and read.
- **ok**: a reputable secondary source, such as national press, a museum or UNESCO listing, or an RTI/NORC programme brief.
- **weak**: a blog, a vendor or prep site, or my own inference or general knowledge (verify before shipping).

Numbered sources are listed at the end, for example [S1].

The P4–6 subject count is inconsistent inside the press release itself. The prose says 11–13 subjects, but the summary table says 10–12 [S1].

**Language of instruction:** in November 2025 the Federal Government cancelled the 2022 mother-tongue policy, and English became the medium of instruction from pre-primary upward [S3] (ok). An English-language bank therefore matches Nigerian classrooms.

**Exams:**

- **NCEE / National Common Entrance** (end of P6; entry to Federal Unity Colleges; run by NECO). The papers are multiple-choice. Press reports list Mathematics, English Studies, Quantitative and Verbal Aptitude, General Science and Social Studies [S4] (ok). The 2026 sitting took place on 6 June 2026, with about 60k candidates and a maximum score of 210 [S4] (ok).
- **Paper structure:** prep sites disagree. One splits Paper I (Maths, English, Basic Science & Technology, National Values) from Paper II (Quantitative + Verbal Reasoning). Another talks of a "General Paper" [S5] (weak). "General Paper" looks like one prep site's own label. Treat it as general science, social studies and civics.
- **Other Nigerian exams:** private and state secondary schools run their own common entrance exams in the same format (weak). At JSS3 there is the BECE (NECO and state). Its subject list was not verified.

- Religious & Moral Education (RME)

The History of Ghana entry is strong. The rest of this list is weak [S7]. OWOP is being phased out, and its strands are being folded into other subjects (2025/26) [S8] (ok).

**Revised KG–P6 curriculum (reported July 2026, rollout date not stated)** [S9] (ok). It names:

- Ghanaian Language, English, French, Arabic and Chinese
- RME
- Mathematics
- Physical Education & Health
- Creative Arts
- Citizenship Education (merges civics, values and history)
- Computing
- Science (with Agriculture as a strand)

Ghana's primary list is in flux, so build Ghana-specific banks last.

**B7–B9 (JHS, CCP).** Every learner studies and is examined at BECE in English Language, Mathematics, Science, Social Studies, Ghanaian Language, RME, Creative Arts, Career Technology, Computing, French and Arabic. NaCCA says there are no optional subjects [S10] (strong). Practice is looser than the policy:

- Ghanaian languages examined include Asante Twi, Akuapem Twi, Ewe, Ga-Adangbe, Dagbanli, Nzema, Fante and Gurune [S10] (strong).

## 2. Which subjects fit a short-answer bank

**Fits well:** one unambiguous answer and plausible distractors drawn from sibling items.

- Spelling: pick the correct spelling, or catch a dictée error.
- Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, meanings, plurals, past tenses.
- Grammar: parts of speech, the correct form.
- Verbal reasoning: analogies, odd one out, word codes.
- Science facts and classification: plant parts, states of matter, body organs, living and non-living, animal groups, food groups, diseases and their prevention.
- Geography: capitals, flags, rivers, regions, landforms.
- Civic facts: arms of government, national symbols, counties and states.
- History: who, what or where (avoid "when" unless the fact is solid).
- French and English vocabulary.
- Computing definitions and parts.
- Agriculture: crops, tools, livestock.

**Fits partly:** comprehension (needs a passage, which is too long for low-end screens and weak readers), Creative Arts (colour mixing, instruments, festivals only), Career Technology and Pre-Technical (tools, safety signs), and Religious Studies (facts exist, but the content is faith-specific and sensitive).

**Does not fit:** essays and composition, oral skills, practicals (science, agriculture, trades), performance arts and sports, school-based projects, and open interpretation.

### Top 10 subject areas to add, ranked

Reach is the share of target children who study the subject. Fit is how well it suits the format. Exam is the subject's weight in national exams. I scored each column myself from Section 1 (weak); the subject facts behind the scores are graded in Section 1.

| # | Area | Reach | Fit | Exam | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | English (spelling, vocabulary, grammar) | All Anglophone countries, all classes | High | NCEE, BECE, KPSEA, KJSEA, LPSCE | Every system tests it, and Nigeria is now English-medium |
| 2 | Basic / Integrated / General Science (incl. body and health) | All five countries | High | All five exam systems | Facts and classification; mostly shared content |
| 3 | Social Studies: geography of Africa and the world | All five countries | High | LPSCE, BECE, NCEE, KPSEA (CA&SS), KJSEA | Maps, capitals, flags and landforms are atomic facts |
| 4 | Verbal Reasoning | Nigeria P4–6 (+ private-school entrance) | Very high | NCEE, the largest motivator | Synonym, antonym and analogy items work well in this format |
| 5 | Quantitative Reasoning | Nigeria P4–6 | High (generator) | NCEE | Number patterns and sequences; extend the maths generators |
| 6 | National history and civics (per country) | All five countries; Nigerian History compulsory P1–JSS3 | Medium-high | NCEE, BECE, KJSEA, LPSCE | Must be per country; sensitivity care needed |
| 7 | Health, hygiene, nutrition and PHE | All five countries | High | Inside science papers | Malaria, handwashing, food groups |
| 8 | Digital literacy / Computing | Nigeria P4+, Ghana B4–B9 (BECE), Kenya Pre-Tech | High | BECE | Definitions and parts of a computer |
| 9 | French (Anglophone) / English (Francophone) vocabulary | Ghana JHS compulsory; Nigeria optional | High | BECE (French), BEPC (anglais) | Pure vocabulary |

Just outside the top 10 are Religious Studies, Kiswahili and the local languages. RE has huge reach and is examined (BECE RME, KJSEA RE), but it splits by faith and is sensitive, so build it only with faith-specific reviewers. Kiswahili reaches one country only. Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba and the Ghanaian languages need native authors and diacritics.

## 3. Common ground: one bank for all, or one per country

### Write once for all countries (English; translate later for Francophone)

- **Basic science:**
  - Parts of a plant and their jobs
  - Living and non-living things
  - States of matter
  - Human body organs and senses
  - Teeth
  - Food groups and balanced diet
  - Germs, handwashing and malaria prevention (mosquito net, standing water)
  - Sources of water, simple machines, magnets
  - Weather, sun and moon
  - Animal groups (mammals, birds, insects…)
  - Domestic and wild animals
- **Geography of Africa:** countries, capitals, flags, major rivers (Nile, Niger, Congo, Zambezi, Volta), lakes (Victoria, Chad, Volta), deserts (Sahara, Kalahari), mountains (Kilimanjaro), regions (West, East and so on), neighbours, oceans and continents.
- **English:** spelling of high-frequency and grade-level words; plurals, past tense, opposites, synonyms, parts of speech, punctuation, prepositions and common errors. Use British/Commonwealth spelling, which all four Anglophone systems use (weak but safe).
- **Verbal reasoning:** synonyms, antonyms, analogies, odd one out, word families, alphabetical order.
- **Quantitative reasoning:** sequences, missing numbers, number boxes (generator).
- **African history (careful wording):** kingdoms and famous people; see Section 4.
- **Computing:** computer parts, input and output devices, basic internet safety.

### Must be per country

- Civic education: arms of government, constitution, national symbols, anthem, pledge, states, regions and counties.
- National history: Nigerian History; History of Ghana; Liberia's founding and the people of Liberia; Kenya's independence struggle; CI and Senegal history.
- Local languages: Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba; Twi, Ewe and the other Ghanaian languages; Kiswahili; Wolof and others.
- Religious studies: CRS, IS, RME, CRE, IRE, HRE.
- Currency and money: naira, cedi, Liberian dollar, Kenyan shilling, CFA franc. This is a good maths-generator skin per country.
- Local geography: states, regions, counties and their capitals.

## 4. African history and geography content for children

### Kingdoms and empires (safe framing, with care points)

| Kingdom | Safe facts for a child | Contested or avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Ghana Empire | Soninke people; West African gold and salt trade; north of modern Ghana (Mali/Mauritania); modern Ghana took its name from it, but the two share no direct historical connection [S24] (ok) | Founding date (the Met gives "c. 300?–1075" with a question mark) [S24]; whether the Almoravids conquered it in 1076 is debated by historians (weak); avoid dates |
| Mali Empire | Founded c. 1235 (Sundiata Keita); larger than western Europe; Timbuktu; Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca (1324) [S24] (ok, the 1324 date is weak) | "Mansa Musa was the richest person ever" is unverifiable; Abubakari II's Atlantic voyage is legend |
| Songhai Empire | Capital Gao; Sonni Ali (rose 1464); Askia Muhammad; Timbuktu and Jenne as centres of learning; fell to a Moroccan invasion in 1591 [S24] (ok) | Spelling varies: Sonni/Sunni Ali, Songhay |
| Kanem-Bornu | Around Lake Chad; one of the longest-lasting states; Idris Alooma (weak) | Dates and dynasty details vary between sources; keep to the place and the Lake Chad link |
| Benin Kingdom (Edo) | Ruled by the Oba from Benin City since about 1300 [S25] (ok); famous for brass and bronze plaques; British forces raided Benin City in 1897 [S25] (ok) | Not the same as the Republic of Benin, which makes a good teaching item; the number of looted objects varies (3,000 to 10,000+), so avoid numbers; restitution is current politics |
| Oyo Empire | Yoruba; ruler titled the Alaafin; cavalry; Old Oyo (weak) | Oduduwa and Ife origins are tradition, so word them as "Yoruba tradition says…" |
| Asante (Ashanti) | Kumasi; the Golden Stool; Osei Tutu and Okomfo Anokye; Yaa Asantewaa led resistance in 1900 (weak) | The Golden Stool's origin is tradition, so word it that way |
| Aksum | Northern Ethiopia; about the 1st–13th centuries AD; giant stone obelisks (stelae); a major trading power [S26] (ok) | Queen of Sheba and Ark of the Covenant links are legend, so avoid them |
| Great Zimbabwe | Stone city built about 1100–1450 AD by ancestors of the Shona; a trading centre; it gave Zimbabwe its name [S27] (ok) | The Queen of Sheba link is legend [S27]; colonial-era "foreign builders" myths must never appear in distractors |

### Independence dates

These are standard reference facts. I did not open a primary source for them (weak). Verify each against a government or UN source before shipping.

| Country | Date |
|---|---|
| Ghana | 6 March 1957 |
| Nigeria | 1 October 1960 |
| Côte d'Ivoire | 7 August 1960 |
| Senegal | 20 August 1960 |

- **Senegal:** the national day is 4 April. The dates differ because of the Mali Federation, so ask "national day" or skip.
- **Liberia:** say "independent since 1847". Avoid "never colonised" and "Africa's oldest republic" framings, which are contested by the indigenous-versus-settler history.

### Notable people (weak, general knowledge)

Prefer historical figures, and avoid current office-holders because they change.

- **Nigeria:** Nnamdi Azikiwe, Tafawa Balewa, Obafemi Awolowo, Ahmadu Bello, Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti, Wole Soyinka (Nobel 1986), Chinua Achebe.
- **Ghana:** Kwame Nkrumah, Yaa Asantewaa, Kofi Annan.
- **Liberia:** J. J. Roberts, Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, Leymah Gbowee.
- **Kenya:** Jomo Kenyatta, Wangari Maathai.
- **Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal:** Félix Houphouët-Boigny, Léopold Sédar Senghor.
- **Elsewhere:** Nelson Mandela.
- **Queen Amina of Zazzau:** partly legendary, so write "remembered as a warrior queen".

**Wording for leaders:** do not ask "Who was Nigeria's first president/leader?". Azikiwe was Governor-General and then ceremonial President, while Balewa was Prime Minister. Ask "first Prime Minister" instead.

### Contested or volatile facts: avoid, or word them carefully

- **Number of African countries:** 54 is the UN member count. The African Union has 55 members because it includes the Sahrawi Republic (Western Sahara) [S28] (ok). Ask "How many countries does the UN recognise in Africa?" or skip the question.
- **Capitals with a twist:** put these in the correction note, or avoid them:
  - Côte d'Ivoire: Yamoussoukro (official) vs Abidjan (economic)
  - Nigeria: Abuja since 1991; Lagos was the former capital
  - Tanzania: Dodoma
  - Benin: Porto-Novo vs Cotonou
  - South Africa: three capitals
  - Burundi: Gitega
  - Eswatini, Equatorial Guinea and Egypt: capitals in transition (weak)
- **"Never colonised":** do not use it for Ethiopia, because Italy occupied it from 1936 to 1941. Use "Ethiopia defeated Italy at Adwa in 1896" (weak).
- **"Discovered":** never write "Mungo Park discovered the Niger". Use "explored" or "travelled".
- **Numbers that go stale:** population, mountain heights given to the metre, and the "longest river in the world" debate (Nile vs Amazon). "The Nile is Africa's longest river" is safe.
- **Topics to avoid in a children's bank:** civil wars and coups (Biafra, the Liberian civil wars, 1980), ethnic rankings, and religion-versus-religion comparisons. Teach slavery and the slave trade, which appear in Ghana's curriculum, only with an approved national framing [S6].

## 5. Reading level for P1–P2 (ages 6–7)

### Early-grade reading results (EGRA)

**Ghana, national, end of P2, 2015** [S29] (strong):

- 51.1% scored zero on the English oral passage, meaning they could not read a single word.
- In the Ghanaian languages of instruction, 54.5% to 92.5% scored zero.
- 2% or fewer read with fluency and comprehension.
- When a story was read aloud to them, 81% could not answer a single question in English. In most Ghanaian languages, fewer than half failed.
- On the maths side, number identification averaged 71.5% correct, with only 0.8% scoring zero.

**Nigeria:**

- Hausa, P2/P3, Bauchi and Sokoto (NEI baseline, 2011): 79% could not read a single word [S30] (strong). Most P2 pupils could not read a word of a Hausa story [S31] (ok).
- Nationally, in MICS 2021, only 27% of children aged 7–14 had foundational reading skills (Grade 2-level story plus comprehension). This ranged from 87% in Lagos to 2% in Jigawa. Foundational numeracy was 25% [S32] (ok).

**Liberia:**

- English, end of G2 / start of G3 (EGRA Plus era): 30% non-readers [S30] (strong).
- 2017: 35% of Grade 2 pupils could not read a single word of a grade-level paragraph, with a mean of 14.6 correct words per minute [S33] (strong).
- Comparison schools were still at 14.5 cwpm in 2021 [S33].

**Kenya:**

- PRIMR 2012 baseline (Nairobi, Thika, Nakuru): non-readers in English were 68.1% in Class 1 and 30.4% in Class 2. In Kiswahili they were 73.2% and 34.8% [S34] (strong).
- Tusome endline (2019): only 14% of G1 and 18% of G2 met the English benchmarks, and fewer than 8% met the Kiswahili benchmarks [S35] (ok).

### Implications

My inference from the data above (weak):

- **Text-only questions exclude most P1–P2 children.** One-third to four-fifths of children cannot read a single word by the end of P2, and almost none read with comprehension. Text-only P1–P2 banks would serve the minority who need them least.
- **English audio helps less than hoped outside cities.** Ghana's 81% zero on English listening shows that rural six- and seven-year-olds often don't understand spoken English. Audio is most valuable in the home language, which is a later edition.
- **Numerals and pictures work.** Number identification is near-universal (Ghana: 0.8% zero). P1–P2 should be numerals, pictures and a tiny fixed set of icon-instructions, played with an adult or older sibling. That matches the shared-phone reality.
- **Design rule:** for P1–P2, use zero required reading. Each instruction is an icon plus an optional spoken prompt via the device's text-to-speech (speechSynthesis) when a voice is installed. Offline TTS availability on low-end Android varies, so never make it required (weak).

### Picture question types that suit this format

These mirror the EGMA subtasks [S36] (ok).

- **Count the objects** (1–20): "🐐🐐🐐 → 3", with numeral options or typed numbers.
- **Which group has more?** Two picture groups, tap one.
- **Which number is bigger?** EGMA quantity discrimination.
- **Match numeral to quantity**, or quantity to numeral.
- **Missing number in a sequence:** "2, 3, _, 5" (EGMA missing number).
- **Add or take away with pictures:** "🥭🥭 + 🥭 = ?"
- **Picture comparisons:**
  - bigger / smaller
  - longer / shorter
  - same / different shape or colour
  - odd one out (3 animals + 1 fruit)
- **Shape and colour matching:** picture to picture, no words.
- **For emerging readers (late P2):** picture to word ("🐐 → goat / boat / coat"), and first-letter choice with audio.
- **Emoji vs bundled art:** older Android fonts lack newer emoji. Use pre-2016 emoji, or bundle small SVGs (weak).

**General pitfalls:**

1. Facts are not copyrightable, but expression is. Write every question and correction note yourself.

4. SA spreads to your bank if you copy text.
5. Translations of CC BY stories may carry their own extra credits.

## 7. Recommendation

### Class range

**First class: P1 (Ghana B1, Kenya G1, Liberia G1, CI CP1).** P1–P2 get only numeral and picture items: the existing maths generators plus a picture deck. They get no text banks. Text banks start at P3 (about age 8), where a minority to a majority can decode, depending on the region.

**Last class: JSS3 (Ghana B9, Kenya G9, Liberia G9, CI 3e; about age 14–15).** Every target system ends basic education with a national exam there:

- Nigeria: BECE
- Ghana: BECE
- Kenya: KJSEA
- Liberia: LJHSCE
- Côte d'Ivoire: BEPC
- Senegal: BFEM

Content is still shared across countries at that level. Senior secondary fragments into elective subjects (Nigeria SSS 8–9 chosen subjects [S1]; Kenya pathways) and should stay out.

Use stage labels that map across countries: "Early (P1–2)", "Lower (P3)", "Upper (P4–6)", "Junior Secondary (JSS1–3 / B7–9 / G7–9)". Use them instead of one country's class names.

### First 8 subject banks, in order

My recommendation, based on Sections 1–5 (weak):

1. **English: spelling, vocabulary, grammar** (P3–JSS3). It is in every Anglophone system and every exam, Nigeria is now English-medium, and it fits the format best.
2. **Basic Science, including body, health and hygiene** (P3–JSS3). It is in all five systems and examined everywhere (NCEE General Science, BECE, KPSEA/KJSEA Integrated Science, LPSCE General Science, CEPE éveil). It is almost entirely shared content.
3. **Verbal Reasoning** (P4–P6). It is the NCEE motivator for the first users in Nigeria, has a near-perfect format fit, and reuses the English word lists. Pair it with Quantitative Reasoning as a generator extension.
4. **Africa and World Geography: countries, capitals, flags, rivers, landforms** (P4–JSS3). It is shared and covered by Social Studies in every system and exam. Wikidata (CC0) and Natural Earth (public domain) make it cheap to source accurately.
5. **Nigeria pack: Nigerian History + Social & Citizenship Studies** (P1–JSS3 curriculum; bank from P3). It is compulsory in the new curriculum and appears in NCEE Social Studies. Nigeria is the largest first market.
6. **Liberia pack: Social Studies** (G3–G9). It covers the founding of the state, counties, government, people of Liberia, and Liberia in West Africa. It is one of four LPSCE/LJHSCE subjects, and Liberia is the second first market.
7. **African History: kingdoms, independence and notable people** (P5–JSS3). Write it once for all countries with the careful wording from Section 4. It is a strong motivator ("our history") and feeds Ghana, Nigeria and Liberia social studies.
8. **Digital Literacy / Computing** (P4–JSS3). It is new and compulsory in Nigeria (Basic Digital Literacy, Digital Technologies) and in Ghana (Computing, BECE), and it is part of Kenya's Pre-Technical Studies. Teachers often lack materials, so the app adds the most value here.

**Next tranche:** French vocabulary (Ghana BECE, Nigeria optional), Agriculture & Nutrition (Kenya), the Ghana and Kenya civic packs, a Francophone edition (orthographe/dictée, which is a CEPE paper and an ideal fit, plus éveil au milieu), and local-language banks only with native authors.

**Format work, in parallel with the banks (not a bank itself):** the P1–P2 picture deck, covering count, compare, match numeral, sequence, odd one out and shapes, built on the maths generators.
