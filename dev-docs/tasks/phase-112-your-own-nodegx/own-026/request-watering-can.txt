Build me an app called Watering Can: my houseplants, and which ones need water.

People sign up with an email and a password, log in and log out. Someone who isn't signed in only ever sees the sign-in page. Each person's plants are their own: nobody else can see them or change them.

A plant has a name, the room it's in, how often it needs water (every so many days), a look, notes, when it was last watered, and whether it's thirsty. The rooms are a fixed list: Kitchen, Living room, Bedroom, Bathroom, Office, Balcony. The looks are a fixed list too, each with its own little picture: Fern, Cactus, Monstera, Herb, Succulent, Flowering.

Pages:
- Home: my plants as cards, thirsty ones first. Each card shows the plant's picture, its name, "Kitchen · water every 5 days" (its own room and days), and a pill that says Thirsty or Happy. Tapping a card opens that plant. A button to add a plant.
- A plant's own page: its picture, name, room, how often, notes, "Last watered 30 October" (or "Never watered yet"), the Thirsty or Happy pill, a "Water now" button with an optional note, and its watering history underneath, newest first, each row the date and the note.
- New plant: a form for the name, room (a dropdown), how often in days, look (a dropdown), and notes. Saving takes me to the new plant's page.
- Sign in, with a way to switch to signing up.

Watering is kept as a record of its own (which plant, when, the note). When a watering is saved, the backend updates the plant: last watered becomes then, and it isn't thirsty any more. Every morning at 08:00 the backend goes through the plants and marks each one thirsty when its days since last watering have reached its "every so many days" (a plant never watered is thirsty).

It should look calm and designed, not like a template: greens on a warm off-white, a serif for the headings, rounded cards with soft shadows, and one set of shared styles that every page uses. It must work on a phone as well as a laptop.

When you're done, sign up as demo@example.com with the password wateringcan1 and give that account six plants spread over the rooms and looks, two of them thirsty and one watered twice, so I can open it and see it working.
