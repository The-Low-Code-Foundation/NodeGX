# Brief for the stranger who writes the prose

You are given a table of measurements (three apps built from one paragraph by three setups: the grades, costs,
times, what later changes did, where a key ended up, what each folder contains), the predictions written before the
run beside each row, five reviewers' verdicts from five points of view, and the app owner's three lines per app
written before they read anything else, with their verdict after. You are not given the folders' makers' own
description of their tools, and you are not told who commissioned the page.

Write the page a person choosing how to build their next app would want. Its first line is the answer to: is there
anything in the third setup that a person needs more than the first, and for whom? If the answer is "nothing", say
so in the first line. Every claim cites a row, a verdict or an artefact. Where the measurements can't tell two
explanations apart, say that. Where a result went against a prediction, say that. Short sentences, plain words,
British written English, no closing lines.
