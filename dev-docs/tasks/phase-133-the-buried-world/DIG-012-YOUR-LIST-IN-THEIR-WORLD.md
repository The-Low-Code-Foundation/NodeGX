# DIG-012 — Your list in their world

**Phase:** [133](README.md). **Depends on:** DIG-008, DIG-009, DIG-011. **Round:** 12. **Scope:**
[SCOPE §8.3](SCOPE.md). **Ruling:** R4's game half.

> "players have to add their own todo items which they'll then be confronted with in the app." · "which you could in
> theory integrate into Dig too at certain lesson points, optional 'what do you think will happen' moments"
> — Richard, 2026-11-03

## What

- **Chambers from tasks:** signed in, the game reads the person's open tasks; heavy, keeping-me-up and big-change
  tasks, and a few others, become sealed chambers in the nearest district, the task in their words on the door.
- **The practice inside** comes from the canon's table (`practices.json`) by the task's kind and how it feels. A table,
  not a model: no AI chooses anything for a person.
- **Asks** from the practice's templates, filled with the task's words. Carving an answer opens a portal (worlds from
  the districts) and **writes the answer onto the task** as a note in the todo app.
- **Prediction moments:** at some practices, the stone offers *If I ___, then ___.* about something coming up. It is
  skippable, it is equal in wording (DIG-011), and it writes a `Prediction` row the todo app shows.
- **The record wall:** a closed task or prediction with its outcome appears, next time, carved into a wall in one of
  their halls: what you thought, and what happened.
- No tasks: nothing is asked; the player explores.

## Acceptance

- **AC1.** A drive: a task marked "keeping me up" becomes a chamber holding the practice its table row names; the
  answer appears on the task in the todo app.
- **AC2.** A skipped prediction moment writes nothing; a written one appears in the todo app's Predictions tab.
- **AC3.** A closed prediction shows on the record wall with both halves, as written.
- **AC4.** A person with an empty list is never asked to add one (every string checked).
- **AC5.** Richard plays with his own list for a week; his words into the README.
